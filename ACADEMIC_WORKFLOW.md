# Evidence-led academic workflow

This is the runtime-neutral method implemented by the workflow engine. Discipline
overlays may add stricter rules but may not weaken these safeguards.

## Stage 0 — Intake and source control

Begin with a Source Intake Gate. Ask the user to provide or identify the material that
should become the registered assignment corpus. Do not treat ambient files, previous
partial intake or other material visible in the surrounding Codex session as complete
or authoritative input. Index only the sources the user explicitly selects.

Locate the exact task, brief, rubric, learning outcomes, submission rules, word count,
citation style, permitted appendices, academic-integrity requirements, case material,
user notes and restrictions on research or AI use.

Hash every file. Treat identical hashes as one source. Treat similarly named files
with different hashes as distinct versions until their differences are resolved.
Preserve originals and record the provenance of derived reading copies and transcripts.
Unreadable material is an evidence gap, not permission to infer its content.

When substantive work resumes after approximately six hours of inactivity, pause at a
Source Freshness Gate and ask whether new or updated assignment/module material is
available. A negative answer resumes the saved phase. A positive answer requires the
user to supply the new material, incremental intake, a file-level change report and an
impact assessment before work resumes. Reopen the briefing, architecture or affected
slices only when the recorded impact requires it.

## Gate 1 — Assessment briefing

Produce a briefing containing:

1. The task in plain English.
2. Every deliverable and required format.
3. The assessment's apparent purpose.
4. Learning outcomes and marking criteria mapped to visible requirements.
5. Word-count and structural constraints.
6. Mandatory concepts, methods, evidence and appendices.
7. The role of the case, client, dataset or personal experience.
8. Citation and research expectations.
9. Ambiguities, conflicts, risks and evidence gaps.
10. The proposed interpretation and decisions required from the user.

Stop until the user explicitly approves or corrects the interpretation.

## Stage 2 — Whole-assignment architecture

After Gate 1, define the central argument, section purposes, exact word budget, rubric
mapping, expected evidence, analytical work, figures, appendices and dependencies on
user input. Turn the architecture into logical vertical slices. Stop for Gate 2.

## Gate 2 — Architecture approval

Present the architecture and its material trade-offs. Drafting begins only after the
user explicitly approves or amends it.

## Stage 3 — Vertical-slice execution

For each approved slice, proceed through:

`PLANNED → RESEARCHED → EVIDENCED → ANALYSED → DRAFTED → VALIDATED → USER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED → INTEGRATED`

Each analytical unit normally makes a claim, supports it, explains the relationship,
applies it to the problem, evaluates alternatives or limitations, and advances the
argument. Research must serve a defined claim or section. Do not retrofit citations
to unsupported prose.

When validation fails, return only to the responsible stage:

- Citation problem → `EVIDENCED`
- Missing research → `RESEARCHED`
- Weak argument or rubric misalignment → `ANALYSED`
- Wrong premise or scope → `PLANNED`
- Writing problem → `DRAFTED`

Autonomous retries are bounded. Repeated failure, missing personal experience or a
material unresolved decision pauses the workflow for the user.

## Stage 4 — Whole-assignment quality gate

Check task and rubric compliance, argument continuity, theory application, evidence,
citations, reference-list correspondence, word-count basis, jurisdiction, uncertainty,
recommendation traceability, authentic user input and document presentation. Render
and visually inspect PDFs, Word documents, slide decks and significant figures.

## Handoff

Provide the deliverable, compliance summary, word-count basis, unresolved caveats and
research record. Submission remains the user's decision and action.
